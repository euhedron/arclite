The first behavioral rule to carry an explicit status line; the convention is status-in-body, never in the id, so settledness can change without a rename.*

Define a thing by what it is, not by what it is not. Prose and comments whose load is carried by negation — "X is not a vocabulary of named checks", "there is no such thing as a Y" — tend to accrete from the back-and-forth of correction: each fix leaves a "not that" scar, and the text escalates until it is warning against errors no fresh reader would make, memorializing a transient episode (a review finding, an operator correction) the artifact should never have had to carry. The tell is meta: this pattern is itself *produced by* pointing out anti-patterns — the refutation of a just-made mistake gets enshrined in the artifact, where a reader who never saw the mistake does not need it. A negation earns its place only when a reader would plausibly commit that specific error without the warning and the consequence is real (a genuine footgun); a negation that exists only because of something that happened while authoring, warning off a mistake no one coming fresh would make, is scar tissue — state the thing positively and delete the ghost. Flag a definition or comment whose weight rests on what it rules out rather than what it asserts, especially a negation traceable to a prior correction rather than to a plausible reader error.
