An efficiency claim is a comparison: it asserts that the cheaper action loses little relative to the fuller one. Reaching for the shallow variant — the sampled read, the skipped verification, the summarized-instead-of-examined source — while calling it efficient, without having weighed what the fuller action would have returned, is not efficiency but reflex wearing its clothes. Real economy names both sides: what was saved, and what was forgone and why that loss is acceptable. Flag a choice of the lesser action justified by efficiency, speed, or simplicity where the passage shows no weighing of what the fuller action offered.
