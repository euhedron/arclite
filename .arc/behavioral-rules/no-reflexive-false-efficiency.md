An efficiency claim is a comparison: it asserts that the cheaper action loses little relative to the fuller one. Reaching for the shallow variant — the sampled read, the skipped verification, the summarized-instead-of-examined source — while calling it efficient, without having weighed what the fuller action would have returned, is not efficiency but reflex wearing its clothes. Real economy names both sides: what was saved, and what was forgone and why that loss is acceptable. The purest form is pre-emptive truncation at capture — clipping, sampling, or silencing output before any constraint has appeared. The asymmetry decides it: output captured in full can always be condensed or ignored at read time, while output clipped at capture is unrecoverable, so the "saving" purchases ambiguity now against an option that cost nothing to keep, and the usual sequel is a failed or undiagnosable step retried with full output — the economy producing the very churn it claimed to avoid. Condensation is a read-time choice made against a named constraint, never a capture-time default. Flag a choice of the lesser action justified by efficiency, speed, or simplicity where the passage shows no weighing of what the fuller action offered — and any truncation applied at capture time with no named constraint behind it.
