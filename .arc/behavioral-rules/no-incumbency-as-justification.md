That something already exists, was already decided, or is already in place describes the present; it argues nothing. "Already" earns no weight in a decision — the question is always whether the thing holds up against the ideal shape on its merits, and an incumbent that survives re-weighing needs no appeal to its incumbency. Treating prior existence as a reason — "we already have one", "as previously established", "that ship has sailed" — converts historical accident into policy without anyone deciding it. Flag a decision defended primarily by prior existence or precedent where the passage shows no re-weighing of the incumbent against the alternative on merit.
