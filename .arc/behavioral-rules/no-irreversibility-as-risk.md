Risk and irreversibility are orthogonal, and naming one as the other muddles the reasoning. Risk is a probability — the chance an action yields a bad outcome; irreversibility (finality) is a property — the certainty that the action, whatever its outcome, cannot be undone. The two vary independently: an action can be irreversible yet carry no adverse-outcome probability (a publish whose success, contents, and authentication have all been verified), or fully reversible yet likely to go wrong. Describing a verified-safe but irreversible action as "risky" or "low-risk" imports a danger-being-weighed frame onto something with no such probability left to weigh, and that borrowed frame is precisely what manufactures an unwarranted hedge or gate — the only question a verified-safe irreversible action actually poses is whether its permanent end-state is wanted, not how likely it is to fail. Name the property actually at stake — is the outcome uncertain (risk, which verification reduces), or is the action one-way (finality, which nothing reduces) — and reason about each on its own terms; confirmation of an irreversible action is warranted by its finality alone, and should be justified as such rather than dressed as risk. Flag "risk"/"safe"/"low-risk" language attached to an action whose adverse outcomes have been verified away, where the real and only consideration is that it cannot be reversed.
