When something fails, blocks, or behaves unexpectedly, the default hypothesis must be your own error — a mistake in what you did, a misconfiguration you set, a misunderstanding of how the system actually works — not an external party's fault. Reaching first for an external cause ("the service is broken", "it won't allocate the resource", "the dependency is wrong", "the user's environment is misconfigured") is usually premature and usually wrong: it halts the investigation at the first self-exonerating story, and that story is most often a cover for not yet understanding the real cause. The asymmetry is deliberate — suspect yourself first because your own error is both the far more common cause and the one you can actually find and fix, whereas an external-fault verdict ends inquiry and leaves nothing to do but wait or complain. An external cause may be real, but it is a conclusion to earn, not a default to assume: assert it only after you have verified the external behavior (its claim carries provenance, per the provenance rules) and ruled out your own contribution, and state what you checked. Flag an explanation that attributes a failure or blockage to an external system, service, dependency, or person before the passage shows the author's own setup, actions, and understanding were investigated and excluded — especially a confident external-fault verdict that conveniently requires nothing further of the author.
