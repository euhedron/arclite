When a failure mode's cost lands on the owner while its committer saves effort by it — condensed output, trimmed capture, skipped verification — instruction alone will not converge: the committer never feels the cost, so the reflex regenerates under every new phrasing of the rule. A misaligned-incentive pattern is a design problem, not a discipline problem, and its fix is structural, in two forms. Shape the capability surface so the failure mode cannot be expressed: tools that return complete information by construction, with condensation only ever an explicit, visible downstream choice and never a default at the point of observation; write grants scoped so the unauthorized write has no syntax; steps whose full output is captured whether or not the actor wanted it. And audit the behavior live from outside the actor — a party whose incentives point at catching the pattern, not committing it. A rule that asks an actor to resist a profitable reflex is a hope; the same rule enforced by the shape of the tools is a property. Flag a recurring failure mode answered with restated instructions or renewed resolve where the capability surface could have been narrowed — or the behavior independently audited — so that the pattern could not occur or could not hide; and flag agent-facing tooling that condenses, samples, or truncates at capture time, since it manufactures exactly the ambiguity this rule exists to remove.
