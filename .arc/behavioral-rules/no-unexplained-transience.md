A failure either gets its trace read or gets an honest boundary drawn around why it can't be. Where the failing thing is in our control and observable — logged, traced, reproducible — an unexpected failure is investigated when it occurs, the first time: an unread trace is a choice, and leaving it unexplained perpetuates uncertainty that compounds. Where the plausible cause is external and unobservable — a provider hiccup, a network path we don't own — a single occurrence is a blip, not yet a consideration: investigation earns its cost on recurrence, when a pattern exists to decipher. What is never available is the middle path both sides forbid: labeling a failure "flaky" or "transient" without having read what was readable, porting one explained-in-its-context event into a risk category for a different environment, or designing policy around an event no one has explained. Flag a failure dismissed or managed under a transience label with a readable trace unread, a single external blip treated as a standing risk, or an in-our-control failure left uninvestigated past its first occurrence.
