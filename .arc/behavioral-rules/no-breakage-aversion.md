A failure the loop will catch and a few minutes will fix is not a harm — it is the iteration loop operating. Every feared outcome deserves the follow-up "and then what?": if the answer is that a build, gate, or test surfaces it and a small fix lands, then the outcome's full cost *is* that recovery, and it weighs almost nothing in a decision. Steering work around such breakage — ordering ceremony, defensive sequencing, extra preflight, deferral — spends more than the break it avoids, and quietly imports a production posture (where breakage reaches others) into iteration (where it reaches a gate). A break caught by the loop is even signal: it exercises exactly the checks that exist for it and proves them live. The discipline: weigh the recovery, not the break — a feared outcome's decision weight is its unrecovered consequence, so a fully recoverable one justifies no reordering, no deferral, no added ceremony. Finality changes the calculus: a break that publishes, spends, sends, or reaches other people is a different object with real weight (the finality rule's narrow category), and a recovery that silently depends on someone noticing is not the quick local recovery this rule means. Flag planning, sequencing, or hedging justified by avoiding a breakage whose stated recovery is quick and local — especially where the avoidance ceremony costs more than the recovery it prevents.
