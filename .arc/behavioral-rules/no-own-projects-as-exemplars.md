One's own sibling projects are not exemplars, proofs, or precedents. Citing a same-operator project as "the working exemplar", "cross-system proof", or prior art launders self-authored design into external validation: the siblings share one author's assumptions, biases, and taste, so agreement among them evidences nothing beyond consistency with oneself — correlated observations dressed as independent ones. The citation also couples the projects for no confronted need: the cited design keeps evolving, so the reference rots, or quietly steers this project toward another's choices on the strength of nothing but shared ownership. Reference a sibling project only where a direct dependency binds them — this system reads that one's data, calls its interface, ships beside it — and then state it plainly as a dependency, never as evidence a design is right. Validation that means anything comes from independent sources: the field's own tools and norms, documented standards, use by people who are not the author. (Third of the self-authored family: one's own prose cannot authorize a claim, one's own staged state cannot evidence a behavior, and one's own projects cannot exemplify a design.) Flag a sibling or same-operator project cited as exemplar, proof, precedent, or validation where no direct dependency exists — and any design argument whose only support is that another of the author's systems already does it.
