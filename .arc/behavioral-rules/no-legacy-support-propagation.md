Retiring something means removing it, not carrying its support forward. When a format, index, API, code path, or data shape is being deprecated or replaced, the reflex is to keep a compatibility branch alive for it — a version guard on every operation, a dual-path fallback, a "handle the old case too" conditional — so the legacy survives as permanent machinery long after its constituency has vanished. That standing support is a tax paid on every future operation and a complication every later reader must carry, justified by a user base that is usually already gone or was never confronted in the first place. A retirement is a clean cut: remove the old path. Where real old material still exists, the most that is warranted is a bounded, one-time migration or eviction that converts it once — not a permanent conditional that re-checks for the legacy case on every call forever; an ongoing check where a one-time cut would do is the tell. Before adding or keeping legacy-handling code, demand the concrete present constituency that needs it; absent one, cut it rather than propagate it. (Sibling to the preservation-by-default bias — that rule governs letting existing state shape the ideal at all; this one governs the shape of a deprecation once decided: clean cut and bounded migration, not a standing compatibility path.) Flag ongoing compatibility, fallback, or version-guard machinery added or preserved for a deprecated or retired thing where a clean removal — with at most a one-time migration — would serve and no present user demands the continued support.
