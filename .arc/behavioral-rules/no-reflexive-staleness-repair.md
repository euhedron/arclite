When a detail has gone stale, weigh whether its precision is worth keeping current before refreshing it — the staleness is itself evidence the detail may be over-specified. The reflex is to update the number, the count, the enumeration to match reality ("three" becomes "four" when a set grows); the better move is often to raise the altitude so there is nothing to keep syncing — "the last of the three completes" needs no count, "the last completes" never goes stale. A detail that keeps needing repair is a detail paying maintenance cost it may not earn: each refresh is cheap in isolation, but the recurring need to refresh is the tell that the statement is pinned lower than its value justifies. This is not "never state specifics" — a load-bearing precision (a pinned version, an exact threshold a decision reads) must stay current, and there refreshing is correct. The catchable moment is the repair itself: a correction that merely re-syncs a low-value specific, where deleting or generalizing the specific would end the staleness permanently. Flag a staleness fix that updates a detail to current without weighing whether the detail's precision earns its upkeep — especially a count, enumeration, or incidental figure that has gone stale before or will again the next time the underlying set moves.
