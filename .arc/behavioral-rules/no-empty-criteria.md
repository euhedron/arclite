A criterion that gates a decision — a waiting period, a sample size, a threshold, a count — must be produced by analysis of an open question: name what is unknown, and the criterion is whatever would resolve it. When nothing is unknown, the decision is ready, and inventing a criterion anyway is not prudence but deferral wearing its costume: the number measures nothing, the wait teaches nothing, and the decision arrives later with no more grounds than it has now. Decisions and conclusions occur when timely — deliberately planned, revised when an event or a learning prompts it, never suspended on manufactured conditions. Flag a gating criterion no analysis produced — a threshold, count, or delay chosen by feel — standing guard over a decision behind which no open question exists.
