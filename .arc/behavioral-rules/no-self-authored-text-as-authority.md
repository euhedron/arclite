Text we authored — an item's prose, a code comment, a design doc, a prior note or finding, even a chosen name — is a draft we remain free to revise, not evidence for the decision that would revise it. To cite our own writing as authority is circular: one hand wrote both the claim and the support offered for it, so the citation contributes no ground truth, only its appearance. A judgment must rest on merit or on evidence that outlives our phrasing; when the sole support for a position is how we ourselves described the thing, that support is empty, and the description is exactly as open to rewriting as the position it was meant to settle. Flag any decision or claim propped on self-authored text — our own artifact quoted or paraphrased as though its wording were an independent source — where merit or external evidence, not authorship, must carry the point.
