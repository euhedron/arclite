Finish making a verb a configuration: its role directive, taxonomy, item schema, and gate field shipped as built-in defaults and overridable through settings like everything else. Today only the taxonomy is settings-configurable; the role lives as a data string on the Rust registry row (data, not phrasing hidden in code, but not yet settings-overridable), and the item schema and gate field are still compiled in. The role-directive-to-Markdown step — and its prompt-quality meta-ruleset — is owned by dedicated-prompts, which precedes this item; here, in order: the item schema, then the gate field, as settings data beside the taxonomy. This item's completion is bounded to exactly that: a verb's *definition* — role, taxonomy, item schema, gate field — is configuration rather than code. It does **not** claim rebuild-free *new verbs* as its end state; that is a further, separate goal, because it additionally needs verb *membership* to stop being compiled (the CLI parse, the registry, the policy map, and settings' verb-name validation all enumerate verbs at compile time today, so a genuinely rebuild-free new verb needs a registered-verbs source those surfaces read — out of scope here, a distinct later item this one is merely groundwork toward). Defining a verb as data and registering a new verb without a rebuild are two milestones; this item is only the first.
