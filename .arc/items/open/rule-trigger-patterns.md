Beyond the prose principle a rule states today, a rule could carry a separate, modular layer of data: the literal words and phrases — and code patterns — that recur in instances of its anti-pattern, kept distinct from the rule's high-level basis and edited/extended on its own, the way taxonomies already are. Two cautions govern whether and how that literal layer feeds a run. First, surfacing the common phrasings an agent reaches for risks biasing the model toward matching them exactly — pattern-patching the listed examples instead of generalizing from the principle, which forfeits the reason to apply AI at all — so inclusion of the literal-example layer in a prompt must be a toggle, exactly so its influence can be measured against the firing-stats rather than assumed. Second, a meta-rule could flag a rule phrased as if it catches only specific instances or examples rather than articulating the principle at a higher level; the `rule-quality` meta-rules already do part of this (catching example- and current-state-binding), and this sharpens that same lens. The upside beyond a guardrail: the same pattern data enables a deterministic, synthesis-free detection mode — literal occurrence-detection of known anti-pattern language, a benefit tier for an operator who will not or cannot spend on AI — and, with both modes in hand, a real experiment: compare the value, cost, and other metrics of literal pattern-matching against AI-enabled auditing of the same ruleset, turning the deterministic-until-synthesis boundary from an assumed division into a measured A/B. The behavioral ruleset is the natural first subject, since reasoning anti-patterns often surface as recurring phrasings — a live session reaching for "dodge", "out of scope", "already" — that a cheap literal pass could catch before the model is invoked at all, complementing the real-time behavioral auditing that ruleset is drafted toward. It rides on rules and taxonomies already being settings-data and on the rule-content model that `dedicated-prompts` and `verbs-as-configurations` develop, so it is placed after them.
